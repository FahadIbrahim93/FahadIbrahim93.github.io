## Post 1 — Monday (hook: the anti-cheat story)
**Voice: technical confidence, short lines**

Building a browser game leaderboard taught me this: if the client can write the score, the score is a lie.

So BugSmasher's client never writes scores. Server-issued one-time tokens, consumed atomically, replay-proof by design.

678 tests including the attack paths. Details: https://fahadibrahim93.github.io/case-study-bugsmasher.html

## Post 2 — Tuesday (hook: rebrand speed)
**Voice: pragmatic, business-aware**

Most e-commerce templates hardcode the business into the components.

RollON takes the opposite bet: one TypeScript config file IS the brand. Change it, and the entire storefront — colors, copy, contact, feature flags — updates. No component hunting.

117 tests, 87% coverage: https://fahadibrahim93.github.io/case-study-rollon.html

## Post 3 — Wednesday (hook: constraint as feature)
**Voice: storytelling, community**

1,700 families. 27 buildings. One grocery market 800m away.

The insight for JG Mart wasn't an app — it was that nobody wanted to download one. So ordering happens in WhatsApp, the PWA works offline after first load, and there's zero app-store friction.

## Post 4 — Thursday (hook: testing philosophy)
**Voice: opinionated**

"Testing slows you down" is what people say before their third weekend of manual regression.

BugSmasher runs 678 tests on every commit. Shipping speed went UP, not down — because confidence compounds. You stop being afraid of your own codebase.

## Post 5 — Friday (hook: AI workflow)
**Voice: honest, no hype**

AI didn't make me a faster typist. It collapsed the boring middle of development — scaffolding, boilerplate, first-draft tests — so I could spend attention on architecture and edge cases.

~60% faster delivery without skipping quality gates. The gates are the point.

## Post 6 — Saturday (hook: accessibility as design)
**Voice: principled**

Accessibility isn't a checklist you run before launch. In BugSmasher it's gameplay: keyboard-first controls, reduced-motion mode, colorblind palettes.

If your game (or app) needs an a11y patch afterward, you designed it wrong from the start.
