⚡ Key Decisions

WhatsApp-first ordering: No app download — meets users where they already are. Direct sourcing: 51% margin by cutting out middlemen, sourced from Krishi Market 800m away. Offline-first PWA: Service worker cache v6 for low-connectivity areas. Cluster routing: 4 delivery zones optimize driver time and fuel. Supabase backend: PostgreSQL + Auth + RPC with Python automation scripts.

01 The challenge

Japan Garden City, Dhaka: 1,700 families across 27 buildings, 4 clusters. One Krishi Market 800m away. Residents needed same-day groceries without app-store friction or middleman markups.

The existing solution was a WhatsApp group with manual order taking — error-prone, unscalable, and impossible to track. The challenge wasn't building an app; it was building a system that families would actually use without downloading anything.

02 WhatsApp-native ordering

The radical insight: don't fight user behavior. Meet it. If families already use WhatsApp for coordination, make it the ordering interface.

  • No app download — ordering happens via WhatsApp message to a business number
  • Structured messages — formatted order templates reduce errors
  • Operator dispatch — admin panel with Supabase Auth + PIN fallback
  • Cluster-based routing — 27 buildings organized into 4 clusters for efficient delivery
  • Order tracking — customers get status updates via WhatsApp
Why WhatsApp-first

App-store friction kills conversion in emerging markets. WhatsApp penetration in Bangladesh is ~40M users. By using WhatsApp as the interface, we remove the biggest barrier: the download.

04 Business model

Not a portfolio demo. A revenue model with real numbers.

  • 51% gross margin — sourced directly from Krishi Market, 800m away
  • 82 products across 10 categories — curated for the residential complex
  • Cluster routing — 4 delivery zones optimize driver time and fuel
  • Zero middlemen — direct sourcing removes traditional markup layers
  • Pre-seed ask — 250K BDT for pilot scale to 5,000 families
Unit economics

Average order value: ~800 BDT. Gross margin: 51%. Net margin after delivery and ops: ~15-20%. At scale (5,000 families), monthly revenue: ~1.2M BDT with ~180-240K BDT net profit.

06 What's next

Pre-seed pilot is live. Scaling requires:

  • Inventory management — real-time stock tracking across categories
  • Delivery scheduling — time-slot selection for families
  • Payment integration — bKash/Nagad for cashless payments
  • Driver app — dedicated interface for delivery personnel
  • Analytics dashboard — sales, margins, delivery metrics
Honest scope

This is a pre-seed pilot, not a Series A platform. The architecture is solid, the model is proven at 1,700 families, and the tech is ready to scale. What's missing is the capital and operational infrastructure for city-wide deployment.

07 What I learned

Building for real users teaches things no tutorial can:

  • Constraints breed creativity — WhatsApp-first wasn't the obvious choice, but it was the right one
  • Business model first — starting with 51% margin forced every technical decision to be cost-conscious
  • Offline matters — in emerging markets, network isn't guaranteed. PWA offline-first isn't a feature, it's a requirement
  • Validation over features — 45/45 HTML and 17/17 Python checks passing means more than a fancy UI
